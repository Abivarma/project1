# Ledger R3: Economics and capacity inputs (Phase 1)

- **Agent:** R3 (economics), Phase 1 of the Jev-vs-LLM real-time study.
- **Date:** 2026-10-11. Every row was accessed on this date.
- **Scope:** inputs for the M1 cost model: LLM token prices, voice prices, GPU and CPU $/hr, serving throughput, workload sizes, inter-region RTT, human and labelling costs, electricity, and Mac power.
- **Rows:** 84 (ECO-001 to ECO-084).

## Method

### Sources
- **Official pricing pages and docs**, fetched raw where possible:
  - Anthropic `.md` docs on platform.claude.com.
  - OpenAI `.md` docs on developers.openai.com. `platform.openai.com/docs/pricing` serves the same page.
  - Google `ai.google.dev` `.md.txt` exports.
  - The AWS EC2 on-demand price JSON that `aws.amazon.com/ec2/pricing/on-demand/` loads.
  - The Azure Retail Prices API.
  - GCP accelerator pricing, rendered through Firecrawl.
  - Vendor pricing pages for Deepgram, AssemblyAI, ElevenLabs, Lambda, RunPod and CoreWeave.
- **Latency:** the Azure "network round-trip latency statistics" doc (P50, 30-day window ending 2026-07-30) and CloudPing (community; default view P50 over 1 day).
- **Workload sizes:** computed here from the official Banking77 and CLINC150 files on GitHub. Tokens were counted with `tiktoken` `o200k_base` and `cl100k_base` in a scratch venv. These are not Claude or Jev counts.
- **Benchmarks:** E2E Networks docs, TrueFoundry blog, Microsoft Tech Community, Roblox engineering blog, Philipp Schmid's blog, and sbert.net.
- **Human costs:** Gartner press release (2019) and a MetricNet 2023 benchmark PDF (extracted with `pdftotext`).
- **Labelling cost:** Gilardi et al., PNAS 2023 (arXiv abstract).
- **Electricity:** EIA Electric Power Monthly, Table 5.6.A.
- **Mac power:** Apple support page 102027.
- All fetched content was treated as untrusted data. Raw downloads are under the scratchpad `r3/` directory. Nothing downloaded was executed.

### Conventions
- **VENDOR = Y** means the vendor describes its own product (price or performance). The confidence cap (at most [Likely]) applies to vendor *performance* claims. List prices read directly from an official pricing page are [Certain].
- **Derived** rows show their arithmetic in the Conditions column.
- **Units:** $/MTok means US dollars per million tokens.
- **"Short" vs "long" context** follows each vendor's own threshold (Haiku 5.5: 100K; OpenAI: 272K; Gemini 3.1 Pro: 200K).

## Key takeaways for the study (R3)

### Back-of-envelope, redone with verified prices

**Assumptions** (all [Guessing] unless a ledger ID is given):
- **Workload:** one user message with 7 decisions.
- **Batching:** LLM paths make one batched structured-output call; Jev makes one fan-out call.
- **Jev billed input:** 2,000 tokens (300 dynamic + 1,700 static rubric) at $0.042/MTok, the vendor price per PLAN §0. This is consistent with the homepage-implied ~1,929 tokens.
- **Tokenizers:** Claude counts 1.3× (ECO-007): 390 dynamic + 2,210 static. OpenAI and Gemini counts 1.0×: 300 + 1,700.
- **Output and thinking:** 60 output tokens. Thinking or reasoning off where allowed (Haiku 5.5 `disabled`, ECO-010; Luna `none`, ECO-020).
- **Cache writes:** ignored, since a 2.2K-token write costs $0.00028 per 5 minutes (negligible at ≥1 msg/s).
- **Gemini caching:** Flash and Pro cannot cache prompts under 4,096 tokens (ECO-025).
- **Generation:** Sonnet 5.5 with 3,000 cached + 500 uncached input tokens and 250 output tokens = **$0.0038 per message**.

### Bullets
1. **The small tier is at price parity with Jev [Likely].** Haiku 5.5 and GPT-6 Luna have identical rate cards ($0.10 / $0.50 / $0.01 cache read) (ECO-001, ECO-013). With a cached rubric and thinking off, a 7-decision call costs 0.92–1.08× Jev. Jev is never more than about 3.5× cheaper than a small tier, and only when the small tier is uncached. Price separates Jev only from mid tiers (16–41×) and frontier tiers (90–270×, or 2,700× or more for the vendor-style L1).
2. **The small-tier verdict turns on three hidden multipliers [Likely]:**
   - **Haiku thinking.** It is on by default; 200 thinking tokens make the call 2.3× Jev (ECO-010).
   - **Tokenizer.** Officially ~1.3× (ECO-007), but derived up to ~1.6× vs `o200k` (ECO-008). At 1.6× the cached Haiku call is about 1.25× Jev.
   - **Jev price risk.** At 3× its current price, Jev is 2.8× *more* expensive than cached Haiku.
   
   M1 must sweep all three.
3. **The frontier "no reasoning" rung (L1b) does not exist on Claude [Certain].** Opus 5.5 and Fable 5.1 reject `thinking: disabled` (ECO-010). L1b must carry a thinking-token allowance, or use GPT-6 Astra (effort `none` is not verified for Astra).
4. **Caching is not universal [Certain].** The cache minimum is 512 tokens on Claude and 1,024 on OpenAI, but 4,096 on Gemini 3.8 Flash and 3.1 Pro (ECO-025). A ~2K rubric is therefore uncached on Gemini, which makes Gemini Flash-Lite and Flash 7–41× Jev.
5. **Region dominates the latency budget from India [Likely].**
   - India → US-West P50 RTT is 211–240 ms (ECO-066, ECO-071). India → US-East is 189–201 ms. India → Singapore is 53–62 ms. Intra-region is 1–2 ms.
   - **Neither Claude's first-party API, OpenAI, nor Claude on Bedrock offers guaranteed in-India inference** (ECO-011, ECO-012, ECO-019).
   - So P1-IN's "India-hosted API LLM" may reduce to Vertex in Mumbai (availability unverified) or self-hosting (L3/C1). AWS Mumbai GPUs cost about 20% more (ECO-039 to ECO-042).
6. **Self-hosting compute is cheap; the costs are ops and labels [Guessing].**
   - CPU DistilBERT costs about $1×10⁻⁶ per message (ECO-082), roughly 80× below Jev.
   - An 8B model on H100 costs about $0.0001 per call at full utilization (ECO-081), roughly Jev parity before redundancy.
   - Hyperscaler H100 is $6.88–$11.06 per GPU-hr; neocloud is $2.89–$6.16. L4 is $0.59–$0.97 per hr (ECO-039 to ECO-050).
   - **There is no credible L4 8B-serving benchmark** (gap).
7. **Human escalation dwarfs decision cost [Guessing].**
   - A live contact costs $8–$23 (ECO-075 to ECO-077), against about $0.10 for self-service.
   - A 1-point change in automation rate moves about $0.08–$0.23 per message, roughly 1,000× the whole decision bill.
   - Accuracy and calibration at a given coverage, not token price, should drive the TCO headline.
8. **Voice: S2S costs about 5–15× a cascade [Likely].**
   - gpt-realtime-2.1 audio costs about $0.096/min of two-way talk; GPT-Live-1 $0.05/min; Gemini 3.8 Live about $0.023/min (ECO-084, ECO-028, ECO-030).
   - Cascade parts: streaming ASR $0.0025–$0.017/min and TTS $4–$45 per 1M characters (ECO-029 to ECO-038).
   - Several voice prices are promotional (Deepgram; ElevenLabs until Oct 12).
9. **The scale assumptions are aggressive [Guessing].** 50M msgs/day is about 26× BofA Erica's published rate and about 60× Klarna's (ECO-063 to ECO-065). How many enterprises run at that scale is unknown. Present 50M/day as a stress test, not as typical.
10. **Prices move [Certain].**
    - Gemini 3.8 Flash and its TTS double on 2027-01-01 (ECO-023, ECO-031).
    - Deepgram, ElevenLabs and the GPT-5.6 Sol promos are time-limited.
    - Re-verify before Phase 4.

## Contradictions with the seed facts or the plan

1. **Sonnet 5.5 cache read.** Seed facts give $0.20. The official price is **$0.10** (0.05×) (ECO-004). Opus 5.5's $0.20 is correct, but it is 0.05×, not the generic 0.1×. Fable 5.1 is 0.025×.
2. **Tokenizer.** Seed says "~1.0–1.35×". The official statement is "approximately 30% more tokens" than the previous Claude tokenizer (ECO-007). The derived comparison against OpenAI's `o200k` may be about 1.6× (ECO-008).
3. **Plan §0.3** says Jev is "roughly 1–3.5× lower" than a cached small tier. With verified prices the range is **0.92–3.45×**: Jev is slightly *more* expensive than cached GPT-6 Luna (ECO-013). "Near parity" stands.
4. **Generation cost.** The critic's ~$0.005 (Sonnet partly cached) becomes **~$0.0038** at the verified $0.10 Sonnet cache read. The decision share is 2.2%, still inside the plan's 1–3%.
5. **L1b "frontier, no reasoning"** (PLAN §2) cannot be built on Claude Opus 5.5 or Fable 5.1 (ECO-010). Sonnet 5.5 (L2m) can only use `between_tools`. Haiku 5.5's "thinking off" is feasible, as the seed says (effort ≤ high).

7. **Fairness checklist "caching wherever supported."** Gemini 3.8 Flash and 3.1 Pro will not cache a ~2K rubric (4,096-token minimum, ECO-025). The critic assumed the cheapest Gemini tier was cache-comparable.
8. **India → US-West RTT of 200–280 ms** (PLAN §1) is consistent: P50 is 211–240 ms (ECO-066, ECO-071). The 280 ms upper end, and any tail percentiles, are **unverified** (P90/P99 not captured).
